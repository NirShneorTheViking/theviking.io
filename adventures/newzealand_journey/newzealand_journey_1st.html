One hike straight into the next

The deep south set the pace for the entire three months: come off one mountain track, resupply in town, follow a gravel road to the next trailhead, and shoulder the pack all over again.

It began in the wet granite heart of Fiordland with three of New Zealand's signature Great Walks back-to-back — climbing over Mackinnon Pass on the Milford Track, traversing the exposed alpine ridgelines of the Kepler, and crossing the Hollyford face along the Routeburn.

From the Routeburn's northern end, the gravel road past Glenorchy led straight into Paradise Valley and deeper into the wild on the Rees-Dart Track — trading Great Walk crowds for braided river flats, glacier-fed valleys, and the steep climb toward Cascade Saddle. Finally, the stage finished back on the road, following unsealed coastal tracks along the windswept sea cliffs and podocarp forests of The Catlins.
